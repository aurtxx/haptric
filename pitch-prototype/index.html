One room.
Two connections.

A school’s internet and a student’s exam shouldn’t share a single point of failure.

Haptric’s idea is simple: the lab computer runs the exam. Students stay connected to it over the local network. The internet connects that lab to the cloud — when available.

This prototype demonstrates that idea in your browser. It does not run a real LAN server or disconnect your network.

More possibility.

A concept for schools and coaching centres where connectivity is a constraint, not a choice.

  1. Before the exam

    Prepare once.

    A teacher creates the paper and assigns the class. The local hub holds what the exam needs.

  2. During the exam

    Keep the room moving.

    Students answer on the lab network. The invigilator sees progress and knows what is waiting to sync.

  3. After the exam

    Bring the records together.

    When the internet returns, accepted events reconcile with the cloud. Conflicts must be surfaced, not silently merged.

Your 60-second story

0–15 seconds. “An internet outage shouldn’t interrupt a room full of students. Haptric separates the local exam from its cloud connection.”

15–40 seconds. Click “Simulate internet outage.” Answer a question. Switch to Control room. “The school keeps the answer; the cloud waits.”

40–60 seconds. Reconnect, finish the questions, and submit. Open Shared receipt and replay synchronization. “The connection returns. The records agree. The classroom kept moving.”
